Where Do I Put It?

Program note

I hesitate to write that this piece is about grief because I risk minimizing the subject (and its discussion in my work) to the trite, controlled (mis)understanding we might all readily conjure: five stages, katabasis, a struggle à la Jacob in Genesis which wins revelation, closure, healing. I resist that. While there is certainly an episodic structure to this work, it's significance lies not in the crystalization of some cliché but in its questioning of the supposed necessity of linearity in grief. Must we relate yesterday to today, before to after, you to the vacuum of your departure? Can we? I wanted to ask, through warping the convention of loss, 'When does the suture destroy what it desperately tries to preserve?'

The Ascetic Worm Beneath Mount Tom

Program note

The star on top of Mt. Tom is a fixture of every winter I spent growing up in Western Massachusetts. During the holidays it still presides over the Pioneer Valley from the site of countless personal memories: family hikes when I was younger, conversations with friends circling the nearby reservoir, plans of scaling Everest, etc. The mountain has always had a mournful quality to me; from the stone ruins of an old hotel which burned down at the beginning of the 20th century (a consequence of the owner's poorly monitored cremation of his several dead horses) to the husk of an amusement park left dormant since the late '80s, the place is perpetually marked in my mind by the tragedy of former greatness. Within this tragedy lies the worm, a paradox of holy asceticism and the low earth, of folkloric magnitude and pathetic vulnerability, of strict discipline and yet claustrophobia. A being inculcated to scarcity who found a Vandermeerean, post-appetite state, the quartet acts as a probe into its frustrated dreams of struggle, pleasure, and self-control.

//

The piece was born out of a melody I discovered while I was folding laundry and ended up burying itself in the final third of the work. From then its composition felt like sculpting outwards from the idea's core, defining a space for the melody to live in. The manifestation of certain material textures and actions by the quartet was instrumental: the metallic shine of coins; the cold stone of sealed caverns; rippling spasms of delicate muscle; the thin whine of air through clogged nostrils; the rattling scratch of disused vocal chords. The piece's near-miraculous momentum with its jilted awkwardness is almost as important as its pitch material, and when it fights to its feet I wonder if it's satisfied.

Bypassing

Program note

In collaboration with dancer and choreographer Lillian Stern, Bypassing is a response to the deluge of modernity – the industrialization of culture, the mangled news cycle tornadoing at unfathomable speeds, the bellows of social media, et cetera – on the paper spirit. An internal spasm of the passionately informed and ineffectual, a raging at and craving for desensitization as a means of both survival and slaughter, a dulling double edge. The work questions what it means (for the individual, for community) to “sneak through the hours. To fall into prostration […] To be accused, by the angel, of being so ready for compromise. To foresee no personal action,” (Etel Adnan, “To Be In Time of War”). Perhaps the impossible and violent project of sense-making in such an episteme is what pulled me towards poetry, particularly Keats’ “Ode to a Nightengale”. His image of Ruth tore through me like a bullet.

RABBIT (film score)

Description

RABBIT was my foray into storytelling through music. As is often the case, I knew not the weight of my decision until I was cartwheeling into the hereafter; working on this project irrevocably changed my narratve and compositional outlook, and for that I'll always be grateful. My friends and I shot the short film during the summer of 2021 in Provincetown, MA.
